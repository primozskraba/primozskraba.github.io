    Research

    I work in applied topology, where algebraic topology meets probability, geometry and computation. These are the main threads, each with a few representative papers.

    Stochastic topology

    What does the topology of a random space look like? I study the homology of random geometric complexes (Poisson and Boolean models, random tilings): the largest persistent cycles, sharp thresholds for the appearance of giant cycles, minimal spanning acycles, and the surprising universality of the \(\text{death}/\text{birth}\) distribution across very different random models.

    Key papers

    • Universality in random persistent homology and scale-invariant functionals Comm. AMS 2026
    • Homological percolation: the formation of giant k-cycles IMRN 2022
    • Central limit theorem for Euclidean minimal spanning acycles J. Topol. Anal. 2024
    • Maximally persistent cycles in random geometric complexes Ann. Appl. Probab. 2017

    Persistence theory

    Persistence diagrams are the central invariant of topological data analysis. I work on their stability, i.e. quantitative control when spaces are only known through samples, and how it relates to classical tools such as exact sequences. I also work on variants of persistence: zigzag, multiparameter, periodic, and Möbius inversion.

    Key papers

    • Möbius homology Trans. AMS 2026
    • Wasserstein stability for persistence diagrams arXiv
    • An approximate nerve theorem Found. Comput. Math. 2018
    • Persistent local systems of periodic spaces arXiv 2025

    Algorithms

    Persistent homology can be computed efficiently, thanks to the hard work of many people. I am interested in its complexity: algorithms in matrix multiplication time, exploiting sparsity, and computing persistence of infinite periodic structures from finite data.

    Key papers

    • Persistent (co)homology in matrix multiplication time SoCG 2025
    • Zigzag persistence in matrix multiplication time SoCG 2011
    • Persistent homology and nested dissection SODA 2016
    • Computing 1-periodic persistent homology with finite windows arXiv

    Topology, data & machine learning

    Topology as a tool for data: incorporating topological information into neural networks, the Euler characteristic as a cheap but informative descriptor, geometric invariants such as metric magnitude and intrinsic dimension, and earlier work on topological visualization of vector fields and time series.

    Key papers

    • A topology layer for machine learning AISTATS 2020
    • Euler characteristic surfaces Found. Data Sci. 2022
    • Approximating metric magnitude of point sets AAAI 2025
    • Persistence-based clustering in Riemannian manifolds J. ACM 2013
